What are Akan day names?

Akan names are given based on the day of the week a child is born. These day names carry cultural meaning and are a powerful expression of Akan identity.

Akan people from Ghana and parts of Côte d'Ivoire use day names as part of their naming traditions. Each name is paired with a specific weekday and is used throughout life as a mark of belonging.

These names are known as "day names" and are an important part of Akan identity and cultural heritage.

Why does the day of birth matter?

Every Akan day name reflects a character trait or a cultural connection that is meaningful in Akan tradition.

Sunday

Male: Kwasi

Female: Akosua

Sunday names are linked to leadership, creativity, and new beginnings.

Monday

Male: Kwadwo

Female: Adwoa

Monday names represent calmness, balance, and peace.

Tuesday

Male: Kwabena

Female: Abenaa

Tuesday names are associated with courage and strength.

Wednesday

Male: Kwaku

Female: Akua

Wednesday names are often linked to introspection and wisdom.

Thursday

Male: Yaw

Female: Yaa

Thursday names represent leadership, reliability, and loyalty.

Friday

Male: Kofi

Female: Afua

Friday names are associated with friendliness, generosity, and joy.

Saturday

Male: Kwame

Female: Ama

Saturday names are linked to strength, intelligence, and stability.

More than just a name

For the Akan people, names carry stories. They preserve family history, cultural values, and a person's connection to their community.

In Akan culture, a name is not simply a label; it represents belonging, heritage, and identity.
